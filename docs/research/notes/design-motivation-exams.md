# Study design, motivation and exam preparation for an adult self-study German app (A2 → B1 → C1)

Scope: adult self-directed learner, no speaking practice, app without streaks/XP/leaderboards. Effect sizes are Hedges' g, Cohen's d or Pearson r as reported. "Single study" marks claims resting on one study.

## 1. Interleaving vs blocking (vocabulary, grammar)

### Takeaway
Interleaving helps when learners must tell similar categories apart (overall g = 0.42). For word learning, blocking did better (g = −0.39). So mix confusable grammar contrasts during review, but don't shuffle new vocabulary across unrelated categories for the sake of it.

### Cited Findings
- Brunmair & Richter (2019, *Psychological Bulletin*): multilevel meta-analysis of 59 studies, 238 effect sizes and 158 samples. Overall interleaving effect g = 0.42. Paintings g = 0.67. Maths tasks g = 0.34. Expository texts and tastes showed no significant effect. Words showed a blocking advantage, g = −0.39. — [Brunmair & Richter 2019, accepted MS](https://www.psychologie.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06020400/2019/Brunmair_Richter_in_press__2019_META-ANALYSIS_OF_INTERLEAVED_LEARNING.pdf)
- Same meta-analysis, meta-regression: interleaving helps more when categories are similar to each other, when items within a category differ, and when material is complex. The authors advise "caution … especially for expository texts and words". — [Brunmair & Richter 2019](https://www.psychologie.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06020400/2019/Brunmair_Richter_in_press__2019_META-ANALYSIS_OF_INTERLEAVED_LEARNING.pdf)
- The "words" category covers concept categories of words, pronunciation rules and translations in different languages (for example Carpenter & Mueller 2013, French pronunciation rules). It is not spaced-repetition flashcard review of L2 vocabulary. Heterogeneity within the category was low (I² = 18.3%). — [Brunmair & Richter 2019](https://www.psychologie.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06020400/2019/Brunmair_Richter_in_press__2019_META-ANALYSIS_OF_INTERLEAVED_LEARNING.pdf)
- No evidence that retention interval moderated the overall interleaving effect. — [Brunmair & Richter 2019](https://www.psychologie.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06020400/2019/Brunmair_Richter_in_press__2019_META-ANALYSIS_OF_INTERLEAVED_LEARNING.pdf)
- L2 grammar: Nakata & Suzuki (2019, *Modern Language Journal* 103, 629–647), "Mixing grammar exercises facilitates long-term retention: Effects of blocking, interleaving, and increasing practice". Interleaved practice felt harder to learners but gave better long-term accuracy. This is a single study; the details below the title were not verified. — [cited in Kasetsart J. Soc. Sci. article](https://so04.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/kjss/article/view/258494)
- Whether interleaving helps L2 grammar is still debated. A 2020s seminar series asked "Interleaving for L2 grammar learning: is mixing structures beneficial?". An SSLA paper reports an interaction between practice type and practice schedule. — [Univ. Toulouse seminar](https://sciences-du-langage.univ-tlse2.fr/accueil/manifestations/seminaire-lnpl-interleaving-for-l2-grammar-learning-is-mixing-structures-beneficial-maud-pelissier-altae-urp-3967-universite-paris-cite); [SSLA: practice type × schedule](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/dynamic-interplay-between-practice-type-and-practice-schedule-in-a-second-language/C01D682D82EDBD00AF29965FCA57BAF3)
- Spacing, which is related but distinct, comes from Kim & Webb (2022, *Language Learning* 72(1), 269–319), a meta-analysis of 37 L2 experiments:
  - Spacing had a medium-to-large effect.
  - Short spacing equalled long spacing on immediate tests but did worse on delayed tests.
  - Equal and expanding schedules performed the same.
  - Effects were moderated by learning target, number of sessions, practice type, feedback timing and retention interval.
  
  — [Kim & Webb 2022 summary](https://pressto.amu.edu.pl/index.php/ssllt/article/view/49487)

### Inferences
- Grammar: block the first introduction of a structure. In later review, interleave confusable contrasts, for example Dativ vs Akkusativ prepositions, Perfekt with *haben* vs *sein*, *weil* vs *denn* word order, adjective endings across cases. This is where between-category similarity is high, which the meta-regression says is the best case.
- Vocabulary: an SRS queue already mixes items, and Brunmair & Richter does not argue against that, because they tested category induction, not paired-associate recall. Avoid deliberately interleaving semantically or formally similar new words at first exposure. Interference risk is plausible but not directly tested here.
- Spacing: the app's SRS scheduler does not need an expanding schedule (Kim & Webb found equal = expanding). Longer gaps matter for delayed retention. Don't let the scheduler collapse into short gaps to fill a daily quota.
- Learners rate interleaved practice as harder. The app should tell them that difficulty is expected (see section 7).

### Gaps
- No effect size extracted for Nakata & Suzuki (2019) or Pan et al. (2019, Spanish verb conjugation). The full texts were not retrieved, so the size and conditions of their L2 benefits are unverified.
- No meta-analysis found specific to L2 grammar interleaving.
- Kim & Webb's overall g was not captured, only the verbal label "medium-to-large".

## 2. Motivation and persistence without gamification

### Takeaway
Gamification raises engagement and gives small-to-moderate learning gains in meta-analyses (g ≈ 0.38–0.49). But rewards tied to simply doing the activity reduce intrinsic motivation in lab studies, and evidence on Duolingo's efficacy is mostly company-affiliated with small or selective samples. The strongest non-game levers with meta-analytic support are progress monitoring that is recorded (d = 0.40) and if-then plans (d = 0.65). L2 identity/vision constructs predict effort strongly but achievement weakly.

### Cited Findings
**Gamification evidence**
- Sailer & Homner (2020, *Educational Psychology Review*) found gamification effects on:
  - cognitive learning: g = 0.49 [0.30, 0.69], k = 19;
  - motivation: g = 0.36, k = 16;
  - behaviour: g = 0.25, k = 9.

  Only the cognitive effect held up in high-rigour studies; the motivational and behavioural effects were unstable. — [Sailer & Homner 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w)
- Bai et al. found g = 0.504 for academic achievement across contexts and a smaller g = 0.377 for language-related disciplines. There was no quantitative meta-analysis solely on foreign-language gamification as of that review. — [as reported in Frontiers/PMC systematic review of gamified FLL tools](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10135444/)
- Deci, Koestner & Ryan (1999, *Psychological Bulletin*), 128 studies: rewards undermined free-choice intrinsic motivation when they were engagement-contingent (d = −0.40), completion-contingent (d = −0.36) or performance-contingent (d = −0.28). Engagement- and completion-contingent rewards also reduced self-reported interest (d = −0.15, −0.17). — [Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999](https://depts.washington.edu/techdocs/papers/deciExtrinsicRewardsAndIntrinsicMotivation99.pdf)
- First RCT of streak highlighting (Aulagnon, Cristia, Cueto & Malamud, IPR working paper WP-26-05, Feb 2026, draft):
  - 60,000 Peruvian 4th–6th graders on a maths platform.
  - Highlighting streaks raised the share of weeks connected by 9.4 pp. Personalised reminders did better at getting students to connect at all.
  - Streaks gave about +0.12 SD on an endline maths test vs control. Only about 1,500 students (2.3%) sat that test.
  - Streak vs other reminder arms was not significant.
  - Baseline use was very low (5.3% ever connected).

  Single study, children, maths, not L2. — [Aulagnon et al. 2026](https://www.ipr.northwestern.edu/documents/working-papers/2026/wp-26-05.pdf)
- The same paper cites Duolingo's own claim that a streak-related change raised Day-7 retention by 14%. This is a company blog figure, not peer-reviewed. — [Aulagnon et al. 2026](https://www.ipr.northwestern.edu/documents/working-papers/2026/wp-26-05.pdf); [Duolingo blog on streaks](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)

**Duolingo efficacy studies**
- Loewen et al. (2019, *ReCALL* 31(3), 293–311): 9 learners of Turkish over a semester. Gains on L2 measures. Moderate positive correlation between time on app and gains. Learners liked the flexibility and gamification but reported variable motivation and frustration with the materials. Small single study by independent academics. — [Loewen et al. 2019](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/recall/article/abs/mobileassisted-language-learning-a-duolingo-case-study/A4D7C8F71782A37D258C19F357DDBCBE)
- Jiang, Rollinson, Plonsky, Gustafson & Pajak (2021, *Foreign Language Annals* 54(4), 974–1002): 225 US adults who completed beginning Spanish/French with Duolingo as their only tool.
  - Reading reached ACTFL Intermediate Low; listening reached Novice High.
  - Reading/listening was comparable to university students after four semesters.

  Several authors are Duolingo staff. The sample is completers only, which brings survivorship bias. — [NAU record](https://experts.nau.edu/en/publications/evaluating-the-reading-and-listening-outcomes-of-beginning-level-/); [Duolingo investor release](https://investors.duolingo.com/news-releases/news-release-details/leading-language-research-journal-publishes-study-showing)
- Smith, Jiang & Peters (2024, *LLT*): 48 independent learners of Spanish over 3 months, about 27 hours. Significant gains on all receptive and productive measures. Duolingo-affiliated, small, no control group. — [Smith, Jiang & Peters 2024](https://www.lltjournal.org/item-detail/1152/)
- Duolingo markets "five sections ≈ five university semesters". This is a company claim. — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-teaches-5-semesters)

**Non-game motivation levers**
- Al-Hoorie (2018) meta-analysis of the L2 Motivational Self System (32 reports, 39 samples, N = 32,078):

  | Component | Intended effort (r) | Objective achievement (r) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Ideal L2 self | .61 | .20 |
  | Ought-to L2 self | .38 | −.05 |
  | L2 learning experience | .41 | .17 |

  Heterogeneity was high. — [Al-Hoorie 2018](https://scholarhub.vn/publication/:slug/a53efe01-7cb6-4bce-8132-3972db2857ed)
- Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006): 94 tests from 63 reports. Implementation intentions ("If situation X, then I will do Y") gave d = 0.65 on goal attainment, including academic goals. — [Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006](https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/publications/implementation-intentions-and-goal-achievement-meta-analysis-effects-and-processes)
- Harkin et al. (2016, *Psychological Bulletin*): 138 RCTs, N = 19,951. Prompting progress monitoring raised goal attainment, d = 0.40 [0.32, 0.48]. Effects were larger when progress was physically recorded and when it was reported or made public. — [Harkin et al. 2016](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/91437/)

### Inferences
- Dropping streaks, XP and leaderboards costs little on learning evidence. Sailer & Homner's cognitive effect is real but modest, and comes from bundles of game elements in mostly classroom settings. Deci et al. warn that rewards for merely completing a session (XP per lesson, streaks) are the type that most reduce intrinsic motivation.
- Streaks do raise usage (one RCT in children). If the app wants that benefit without the loss-aversion pressure, use a non-punitive consistency record instead: "studied 5 of the last 7 days", with no reset to zero.
- Replace game rewards with what has stronger evidence:
  - **Recorded progress** (Harkin d = 0.40): words mature in SRS, Lesen-Teil scores over time, writing word counts, estimated hours against the Goethe hour bands.
  - **Implementation intention prompt** at setup and weekly (d = 0.65): "When I … (cue), I will open Today and do the reviews." Store it and show it on Today.
  - **Ideal-L2-self framing**: the C1 goal, written by the learner. Expect it to sustain effort more than raise scores directly (r = .61 vs .20).
- In self-determination terms:
  - Autonomy = letting the learner choose which Today blocks to do, rather than making them mandatory.
  - Competence = visible improvement on exam-format tasks.
  - Relatedness is weak in a solo app and the no-speaking constraint makes it weaker. A link to the B1 course or a tutor is the realistic route.
- Treat Duolingo efficacy figures as upper bounds. Samples are completers, often company-run, and have no control group.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed estimate of Duolingo or app attrition rates was found. Industry figures such as "churn reduced from 47% to 28%" come from marketing blogs and were excluded.
- No SDT meta-analysis specific to L2 apps was retrieved.
- No study isolating streaks' effect on L2 learning outcomes for adults was found.
- Goal-setting theory (Locke & Latham) effect sizes were not retrieved.

## 3. Self-regulated learning and CEFR can-do self-assessment

### Takeaway
L2 self-assessment correlates only moderately with external proficiency measures: r ≈ .45 in the larger, newer meta-analysis, and .63 in an older, smaller one. Accuracy improves with task-specific, criterion-referenced (can-do/rubric) items and with training. Self-assessment can drive planning and motivation, but the app should not use it alone to judge readiness.

### Cited Findings
- Ross (1998): 10 studies, 60 correlations. Mean r = .63 between self-assessment and other measures; speaking r = .55. "Considerable variation" between learners. — [Language Teaching review, CUP](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/786D4E0E0D500DCDD43CCE24ED610592/core-reader)
- Li & Zhang (2021): 67 studies, 214 correlations, 42 years. Mean r ≈ .45, described as "relatively weak"; speaking r = .44. Correlations were higher for:
  - task-specific self-assessment (.49);
  - criterion-referenced/rubric-based items (.49);
  - learners trained in self-assessment (.48);
  - computer-adaptive formats (.52).
  
  — [same review](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/786D4E0E0D500DCDD43CCE24ED610592/core-reader); [Li & Zhang data, SAGE figshare](https://sage.figshare.com/collections/A_meta-analysis_of_self-assessment_and_language_performance_in_language_testing_and_assessment/5021492)
- Reported benefits of self-assessment: self-efficacy and strategy use (Panadero et al. 2017); agency and "possible selves" in L2. This is cited secondary evidence and was not quantified here. — [same review](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/786D4E0E0D500DCDD43CCE24ED610592/core-reader)
- Recording and reporting progress strengthens goal attainment (Harkin et al. 2016, d = 0.40). — [Harkin et al. 2016](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/91437/)

### Inferences
- Use specific CEFR can-do statements tied to tasks, e.g. "I can find the matching ad for 7 situations, including one with no match, in 10 minutes", not global ones like "I am B1". The specific kind correlate better (.49 vs .45 average).
- Calibrate self-ratings: after each exam-format task, show the learner's prediction next to the actual score. This is effectively the training moderator, and it shows overconfidence.
- Readiness for the B1 exam should come from timed Modellsatz-format scores against the 60% pass line, not from can-do ticks.
- A weekly 2-minute reflection (what worked, what to change, next week's plan) fits the plan-monitor-reflect SRL cycle and the recording effect. No direct effect size was found for reflection prompts in L2 apps.

### Gaps
- No meta-analytic effect size for SRL interventions in L2 self-study was retrieved. Known reviews were not fetched in this pass.
- No data on accuracy of self-assessment against CEFR descriptors specifically for German learners.

## 4. Time allocation: daily dose, distribution, hours per level, four strands

### Takeaway
Goethe estimates 350–650 teaching units of 45 minutes (≈260–490 h) to B1 and 800–1,000 units (≈600–750 h) to C1. From A2 to C1 that is several hundred more hours. Distribute practice: longer gaps aid delayed retention. Nation's four strands suggest about 25% each for meaning-focused input, meaning-focused output, language-focused study and fluency.

### Cited Findings
- Goethe-Institut estimates (cumulative, 45-minute teaching units):

  | Level | Teaching units | Hours |
  |---|---|---|
  | B1 | 350–650 | ≈260–490 |
  | B2 | 600–800 | ≈450–600 |
  | C1 | 800–1,000 | ≈600–750 |
  | C2 | 1,000+ | ≈750+ |

  Learning is faster with prior languages and with immersion in Germany. — [Goethe-Institut London FAQ](https://goethe.de/ins/gb/en/sta/lon/kur/faq.html)
- Cambridge guided learning hours for English (cumulative): B1 ≈350–400, B2 ≈500–600, C1 ≈700–800. Guided hours exclude self-study. — [Cambridge English support](https://support.cambridgeenglish.org/hc/en-gb/articles/202838506-Guided-learning-hours)
- Spacing (Kim & Webb 2022): longer spacing beats shorter on delayed tests; equal = expanding. — [Kim & Webb 2022 summary](https://pressto.amu.edu.pl/index.php/ssllt/article/view/49487)
- Nation (2007): a balanced course has four roughly equal strands:
  - meaning-focused input (reading/listening);
  - meaning-focused output (speaking/writing);
  - language-focused learning (deliberate vocabulary, grammar, spelling, discourse);
  - fluency development (fast use of known material).

  Flashcard-type study should be about 25% of time. — [TESL Ontario on Nation's four strands](https://contact.teslontario.org/?p=2244)
- Loewen et al. (2019) found a moderate positive correlation between time on app and gains. This is a single small study. — [Loewen et al. 2019](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/recall/article/abs/mobileassisted-language-learning-a-duolingo-case-study/A4D7C8F71782A37D258C19F357DDBCBE)

### Inferences
- Rough budget: A2 (≈200–350 cumulative h by Goethe's bands) to C1 (600–750 h) is ≈350–500 further hours, course plus self-study.
  - At 45 min/day of app study plus a course, C1 in about 2–3 years is plausible. At 20 min/day alone it is not.
  - The exam page could show estimated hours logged against these bands. Caveat: they are course hours, not app hours.
- Today page split at B1, applying the four strands without speaking:
  - ≈25% SRS and grammar (language-focused);
  - ≈25–35% reading and listening for meaning (input);
  - ≈15–25% writing (output; the only output strand available);
  - ≈15–25% fluency: timed easy reading, re-listening, fast writing on familiar topics.

  The app risks over-weighting SRS. Cap reviews so that input and writing still happen.
- Daily short sessions beat weekend cramming for retention, and SRS intervals should be allowed to grow.

### Gaps
- No meta-analytic evidence on the optimal daily minutes for adult L2 self-study.
- Goethe's ranges are for classroom courses. No equivalent conversion for app hours was found.
- Nation's 25% split is a design principle, not an experimentally derived ratio.

## 5. Fluency without speaking: timed/speed reading, repeated reading, timed writing

### Takeaway
Speed-reading courses on easy text reliably raise L2 reading rate. Gains depend on very familiar vocabulary and comprehension checks, and transfer to authentic texts is less certain. Evidence for timed writing as fluency training was not found in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- Nation's fluency strand uses familiar language under time pressure. Speed reading meets its conditions. — [TESL Ontario](https://contact.teslontario.org/?p=2244); [Chung 2010, AJELT 20](https://cup.cuhk.edu.hk/chinesepress/journal/AJELT20/AJELT20_95-116.pdf)
- Chung's replication of Chung & Nation (2006) had results that matched the original, and every student increased speed when slow readers were monitored. Obstacles were translating into L1 and negative attitudes to speed training. Small single-site studies. — [Chung, AJELT 20](https://cup.cuhk.edu.hk/chinesepress/journal/AJELT20/AJELT20_95-116.pdf)
- Other L2 speed-reading studies report rate gains of about 13–27 standard words per minute in treatment groups, and at least 38 wpm in another. Three to four sessions a week for 4–5 weeks were suggested. Source attribution among the search results was unclear, so treat these as indicative. — [ERIC EJ1176224](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1176224.pdf); [JALT JER: extensive + speed reading in L2 Japanese](https://jalt-publications.org/content/index.php/jer/article/view/1206)
- Macalister (2010) investigated whether speed-reading course gains transfer to authentic texts. It is described as preliminary; results were not retrieved. — [Reading in a Foreign Language](https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/handle/10125/66649)

### Inferences
- Add a weekly timed-reading block:
  - short, easy texts at about 98% known words, below the learner's level;
  - a fixed number of comprehension questions;
  - a words-per-minute log shown as a chart. This is progress monitoring.
- Discourage translating (Chung): no glossing in fluency mode.
- Repeated listening to already-understood Hören texts and quick-writes on familiar themes (e.g. 10 minutes, no editing) are reasonable fluency activities by Nation's definition. Direct outcome evidence was not found here.
- Exam link: B1 Lesen is 65 minutes for 30 items over five texts. Reading rate is an exam constraint, not just a skill.

### Gaps
- No meta-analysis on L2 timed or repeated reading for adults was retrieved.
- No evidence retrieved on timed writing or "free writing" for L2 writing fluency.
- No German-specific speed-reading norms (wpm at B1/C1).

## 6. Goethe-Zertifikat B1 (and B2, C1): format and test-preparation evidence

### Takeaway
The current B1 has four independently passable modules. Each needs 60/100. Lesen has 30 items and Hören 30 items, converted at ×3.33, so 18/30 correct passes. Schreiben is three tasks in 60 minutes. The rules apply from 1 September 2025 and also cover a digital format. Practice testing is the best-supported preparation, but there is little evidence on test-format familiarity alone in L2.

### Cited Findings
**B1 format** (Durchführungsbestimmungen, in force for exams after 1 Sept 2025)
- Four modules: Lesen, Hören, Schreiben (group exam) and Sprechen (pairs). They can be taken singly or in combination. — [Goethe B1 DFB, Sept 2025](https://www.unifr.ch/centredelangues/en/assets/public/documents/Goethe/DFB_Sept25_DE_EN.pdf)
- Times:
  - Lesen 65 min;
  - Hören ≈40 min;
  - Schreiben 60 min;
  - total written ≈165 min, with breaks of at least 15 min between modules;
  - Sprechen ≈15 min in pairs (≈10 min single) plus 15 min preparation.
  
  — [Goethe B1 DFB](https://www.unifr.ch/centredelangues/en/assets/public/documents/Goethe/DFB_Sept25_DE_EN.pdf)
- Answer transfer: plan about 5 minutes inside Lesen time; about 5 minutes allowed within Hören. — [Goethe B1 DFB](https://www.unifr.ch/centredelangues/en/assets/public/documents/Goethe/DFB_Sept25_DE_EN.pdf)
- Scoring for Lesen and Hören:
  - 30 items each, 1 or 0 points;
  - score = items × 3.33, rounded (18 items = 60, 21 = 70, 27 = 90).

  Schreiben and Sprechen are each marked by two independent raters and averaged. A third rating happens when the two raters fall either side of 60. — [Goethe B1 DFB](https://www.unifr.ch/centredelangues/en/assets/public/documents/Goethe/DFB_Sept25_DE_EN.pdf)
- Grades: 60–69 *ausreichend*; below 60 *nicht bestanden*. A module passes at ≥60 points (60%). Modules can be taken or retaken "as often as" the centre can organise. — [Goethe B1 DFB](https://www.unifr.ch/centredelangues/en/assets/public/documents/Goethe/DFB_Sept25_DE_EN.pdf)
- Digital format: Hören is played through headphones on the platform. Lesen and Hören are auto-scored. Writing and speaking criteria are the same as on paper. Stamped scrap paper is available. — [Goethe B1 DFB](https://www.unifr.ch/centredelangues/en/assets/public/documents/Goethe/DFB_Sept25_DE_EN.pdf)

**Test-preparation evidence**
- Adesope, Trevisan & Sundararajan (2017, *Review of Educational Research*): 118 articles, 272 effect sizes, N = 15,472.
  - Practice tests beat restudy and every other comparison.
  - Mixed-format tests were most effective.
  - Feedback added only slightly.
  - Effects held in real classrooms and were stronger with retention intervals over 1 day.

  — [Learning Scientists summary](https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2017/2/9-1); [WSU PDF](https://education.wsu.edu/documents/2018/01/rethinking-use-tests.pdf/)
- Washback reviews: tests shape learners' content, practices and affect. Negative washback occurs when study shifts from the language to test-taking tricks. — [Springer LTA review](https://link.springer.com/10.1186/s40468-016-0030-z); [TESOL: 35 washback studies](https://my.tesol.org/news/1196429)
- IELTS-prep classes improved scores, but no more than general academic English classes. This was reported in washback reviews; the original study was not verified (likely Green 2007). — [Springer LTA review](https://link.springer.com/10.1186/s40468-019-0096-5)
- Washback research seldom controls for prior test familiarity, so how much format familiarity alone adds is poorly established. — [Language Testing 42(1), 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/reader/10.1177/02655322241249754)

## 7. Learning from errors and desirable difficulties

### Takeaway
Making errors and then getting corrective feedback aids learning, and high-confidence errors are corrected best (hypercorrection). This sits alongside the retrieval, spacing and interleaving evidence. Difficulties are "desirable" only when the learner can eventually succeed and gets feedback.

### Cited Findings
- Metcalfe (2017, *Annual Review of Psychology*): lab studies show errorful learning followed by corrective feedback is beneficial. Error avoidance may be counterproductive for neurotypical learners. — [Metcalfe 2017](https://wesleyan.edu/ofcd/resources/OFCD%20Resource%20Docs/Learning%20from%20Errors.pdf)
- Hypercorrection: errors made with high confidence are corrected more readily than low-confidence ones, plausibly through surprise and attention. — [Metcalfe 2017](https://wesleyan.edu/ofcd/resources/OFCD%20Resource%20Docs/Learning%20from%20Errors.pdf)
- Corrective feedback, including analysis of the reasoning behind the mistake, is crucial. — [Metcalfe 2017](https://wesleyan.edu/ofcd/resources/OFCD%20Resource%20Docs/Learning%20from%20Errors.pdf)
- Retrieval practice beats restudy (Adesope et al. 2017). Feedback adds only slightly on average. — [Learning Scientists summary](https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2017/2/9-1)
- Interleaving helps discrimination of similar categories but not word lists (g = 0.42 overall; words −0.39). — [Brunmair & Richter 2019](https://www.psychologie.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06020400/2019/Brunmair_Richter_in_press__2019_META-ANALYSIS_OF_INTERLEAVED_LEARNING.pdf)
- Spacing helps L2 learning, with longer gaps better for delayed retention (Kim & Webb 2022). — [Kim & Webb 2022 summary](https://pressto.amu.edu.pl/index.php/ssllt/article/view/49487)
- Learners perceive interleaved grammar practice as harder even when it gives better long-term accuracy (Nakata & Suzuki 2019; single study). — [cited in Kasetsart J. Soc. Sci.](https://so04.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/kjss/article/view/258494)

### Inferences
- Ask for production before revealing the answer: type the German, choose the article, fill the adjective ending. Then show immediate correction with a one-line rule explaining why.
- Add an optional confidence tap (sure / unsure) on grammar and SRS items. Flag confident errors for a quick re-test in the same session, exploiting hypercorrection. Feed confidence-vs-accuracy into the self-assessment calibration from section 3.
- Keep an error log: recurring grammar errors from writing and exercises become SRS cards. This turns mistakes into retrieval practice.
- Explain once in the UI that harder-feeling practice (mixed, spaced, recall) produces better retention. Learners otherwise read difficulty as failure and switch back to easier strategies.
- Keep difficulty achievable. Exam-format tasks should start untimed and accuracy-focused, then add the timer.

- Bjork's "desirable difficulties" primary sources were not fetched. The principle is represented here through the spacing, retrieval and interleaving meta-analyses.
- No effect size was retrieved for hypercorrection.
